The short answer

What to use for which job

Sarvam is the specialist pick for Indian speech and for translating into Indian languages. For reasoning over text, documents and code, a price-matched Gemini, OpenAI or Claude model was more accurate in every test.

Speech recognition, Indian languages
Saaras v3. Lowest word error rate in 12 of 13 languages and about 4× faster than Gemini. For calls where amounts and account digits matter, Gemini 3.8 Flash captured them exactly 94% of the time against Saaras's 81%, because Saaras often spells numbers out as words.
Real-time voice bot
All-Sarvam stack (Saaras → Sarvam-105B → Bulbul): first audio in about 1.5 s, replies in the caller's language 19 of 20 times, ₹1.56 per conversation-minute. The all-Gemini stack read numbers back more accurately but took about 6.8 s.
Text-to-speech
Bulbul v3. As intelligible as Gemini and OpenAI voices across 10 languages, with the fastest first audio.
Translating into Indian languages
Sarvam-Translate (best in 8 of 10 languages). Avoid Mayura for anything with numbers. Translating into English: Gemini 3.8 Flash, Claude Haiku or GPT-5.4 mini, all well ahead of Sarvam.
KYC, invoices, land records
Gemini 3.8 Flash or Claude Sonnet 5.5 on the image. Clean scans are a tie at about 99.5% for everyone; on bad phone photos Sarvam Vision invented fluent text that was not on the page, and OpenAI models misread Indian-script names.
Chat, Q&A, business text
Gemini 3.8 Flash or GPT-5.4 mini. Sarvam-105B gets close only with thinking on, which costs 5–20 s per answer and sometimes returns nothing.
Clinical notes
Gemini 3.8 Flash, on the text or straight on the audio. No cloud model invented a medicine in 40 consultations.
Coding agents
Gemini 3.8 Flash passed all 160 task-language items first try, Claude Haiku 5.5 99% and GPT-5.4 mini 92–98%. Sarvam-105B passed 56–69%, and none of the Aadhaar-checksum tasks.
Data must stay on your servers
Qwen3-30B over Sarvam-30B for accuracy (84% vs 63% on business tasks). Sarvam-30B is faster per token and much cheaper to feed Indian text, but ignores format instructions. Neither refuses out-of-scope questions without thinking on.

What cuts across the twelve tests

Five patterns

1. Sarvam-105B's thinking mode is a gamble

With thinking off (reasoning_effort: null) Sarvam-105B answers in about 0.5–1.5 s. With thinking on it is more accurate on structured tasks, but answers take 5–46 s and the reasoning length is unpredictable: 29% of coding calls and 16 of 80 legal summaries came back empty because reasoning used the whole token budget, and you still pay for those tokens. Setting "low" did not reduce thinking. Leave at least 16k output tokens of headroom if you turn it on.

2. Self-hosted Sarvam-30B knows the facts but not the format

Sarvam removed Sarvam-30B from its API, so self-hosting the open weights is the only way to use it. On this Mac it ran faster than Qwen3-30B (about 27 vs 19 tokens/s) and needed less memory, but it labelled Tamil, Telugu and Bengali tickets as "Hinglish", wrote CRM notes in the wrong language, appended unrequested explanations, and invented answers to out-of-scope questions. Its thinking cannot be switched off through the chat API; only pre-filling an empty <think></think> works.

3. Sarvam's tokenizer is the cheapest way to send Indian text

The same sentence costs 1.55× its English token count on Sarvam's tokenizer, 1.89× on Gemini's, 2.10× on OpenAI's, 3.44× on Claude's and 5.89× on Qwen3's. Sarvam's vocabulary is Gemma's with about 22,800 slots re-assigned to Indian scripts, so the saving is largest on Odia, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Gujarati and Punjabi.

4. Under stress, models invent rather than leave blanks

On badly photographed documents Sarvam Vision wrote fluent text that was not on the page, and GPT-5.5 invented firm names and GST numbers. Sarvam-105B without thinking added deadline dates and totals it had computed itself to 9% of legal summaries. Every cloud model refused all ten out-of-scope farming questions; both self-hosted models invented answers such as 150 kg of DAP per acre for a crop the sheet never covered.

5. Answering in the user's language is still unsolved

Asked in English about a Hindi or Tamil document, most models answered in the document's script: GPT-5.4 mini replied in English 7% of the time and Sarvam-105B 11%. Romanised questions (Hinglish, Tanglish) got a romanised reply from no price-matched model more than 69% of the time. Only the frontier models matched the user every time.

Method

How it was run

Six parallel agents built and ran the twelve suites on one Apple M5 Max (128 GB) between 19:30 and 23:30 IST on 11 October 2026, sharing one Python library for API clients, scoring and result files. Systems were called through their official APIs; Claude through OpenRouter; Sarvam-30B and Qwen3-30B through llama.cpp on the same Mac.

  • Known answers only. Every item has a gold answer (a number, code, label, fact list or reference text) and is scored by code. No model judges another's free text. Where speech or translation quality needed a reference, public datasets (FLEURS/FLORES, CC-BY 4.0) or round-trip transcription by two or three different vendors' transcribers were used.
  • Mostly synthetic, realistic data. Invoices, KYC forms, WhatsApp orders, policies, consultations and legal passages were written for this benchmark, with fake people and clearly marked sample IDs. None were reviewed by native speakers; treat absolute scores as indicative and the comparisons as the result.
  • Same inputs, both modes. Sarvam-105B ran with thinking off and on; Gemini Flash with thinking off (and on where it mattered). Frontier models (Gemini Pro, GPT-5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5) ran on fixed 30-item subsets to show the ceiling.
  • Uncertainty shown. 95% bootstrap confidence intervals on every score; hover any bar for its interval and sample size.
  • Sandboxed code. Model-written code ran only inside a macOS sandbox with no network, no reads of the home folder and no writes outside a temporary folder.
  • Resumable and auditable. Every model output is kept in results/raw/*.jsonl; reruns only call the API for missing items.

For developers

API quirks we hit

  • Sarvam-30B returns "deprecated" on the API; only sarvam-105b and sarvam-105b-conversations remain.
  • Sarvam-105B: reasoning_effort: "low" still spent about 1,300 reasoning tokens on a one-line task; null turns thinking off.
  • Sarvam-105B thinking can return content: null after spending the whole budget, with no visible reasoning either.
  • Out of credits: chat returns HTTP 402 insufficient_quota_error; Sarvam Vision's /start returns HTTP 400 "Insufficient credits", so a 402-only check misses it.
  • Sarvam Vision accepts only PDF or ZIP uploads (wrap an image in a one-file ZIP) and returns a ZIP with markdown whose tables are HTML.
  • Saaras batch names output files 0.json, 1.json… by upload order, so two jobs downloaded into one folder overwrite each other. Map outputs through the job status instead.
  • The Saaras batch endpoint is documented two ways; /speech-to-text/job/v1 with a job_parameters body works.
  • Saaras codemix mode returned no Latin letters at all in 30% of outputs, and wrote numbers as words ("बारह हज़ार").
  • Saaras writes Sindhi in Devanagari only; FLEURS and most Sindhi users expect Perso-Arabic script.
  • Bulbul's REST response holds audio in an audios list; its streaming endpoint delivered complete audio about 3× faster than REST.
  • Odia's language code is od-IN. Mayura accepts at most 1,000 characters per request.
  • Sarvam's chat endpoint accepts OpenAI-style tools and returns tool_calls.
  • Sarvam API prompt_tokens equals the open tokenizer's count plus 14 tokens per request.
  • Sarvam-30B GGUF ignores enable_thinking: false, reasoning_effort and <|nothink|>; prefill an empty <think></think> via /completion.
  • Gemini 3.8 Flash refused 3–6% of harmless audio clips with BlockedReason.OTHER, and still reported thought tokens on most calls with thinking_budget: 0.
  • gemini-embedding-2 does not support task_type, and a list of strings in one request returns one merged embedding.
  • Gemini Pro and Claude Sonnet 5.5 cannot turn thinking off.
  • Gemini TTS multi-speaker needs speech_metadata.speaker on every text part; a plain "Doctor: …" script returns HTTP 400.
  • OpenAI whisper-1 rejects bn, ml, te, gu and pa as language hints; gpt-4o-transcribe rejects pa and or.
  • Through OpenRouter, Claude's content filter blocked four ordinary news sentences (one about Ebola) with no token usage returned.
  • A Gemini Python client call can hang indefinitely without an explicit HttpOptions(timeout=…).
  • OpenAI rejects function tools combined with reasoning_effort on /v1/chat/completions for GPT-5.4 mini; use /v1/responses.
  • On /v1/responses, function tools default to strict: true, making every argument required: GPT models then sent amount: 0 for "pay the full bill" (74% exact). With strict: false they scored 100%.

Cost

What the benchmark cost to run

About ₹1,200 of Sarvam credit at list prices (₹1,500 topped up), a few US dollars of OpenAI usage (recorded as tokens), US$3.75 on OpenRouter for Claude, and a few million Gemini tokens. Sarvam's translation APIs were the single biggest line: about ₹300 for 150,000 characters of legal text, which Sarvam-105B translated just as faithfully for about ₹2. List prices used: speech-to-text ₹30 per audio hour (₹45 with speaker labels), Sarvam-105B ₹15 / ₹60 per million input / output tokens, Bulbul ₹30 per 10,000 characters, Sarvam Vision ₹0.50 per page, translation about ₹20 per 10,000 characters.

Reproduce

Run it yourself

git clone https://github.com/shivanathd/sarvam-field-test
cd sarvam-field-test
cp .env.example .env        # SARVAM_API_KEY, GOOGLE_API_KEY, OPENAI_API_KEY, OPENROUTER_API_KEY
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
scripts/serve_local.sh      # optional: self-hosted Sarvam-30B and Qwen3-30B via llama.cpp
.venv/bin/python suites/uc08_quick_commerce/run.py api   # any suite; reruns skip finished items
.venv/bin/python report/build_report.py                  # rebuild this page from results/

Everything is open source at github.com/shivanathd/sarvam-field-test: every script, test set, result and raw model output, so the report rebuilds without any API key.
